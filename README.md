DREAMSTREAM

What if YouTube streamed worlds instead of videos?

---

The Vision

We spend a significant part of our lives asleep.

During those hours, the human mind can enter worlds that feel real, strange, beautiful, frightening, or completely impossible.

But dreams are usually unpredictable.

They are often fragmented, personal, difficult to navigate, and experienced alone.

What if we could make dreams and immersive experiences more intentional?

What if, instead of simply closing our eyes and disappearing into an unpredictable internal world, we could eventually enter worlds we choose, explore them, and share experiences with other people?

DreamStream is an attempt to explore that possibility.

---

The Idea

Imagine putting on an immersive headset and saying:

«"Take me to a quiet beach in New Zealand at sunset."»

Instead of opening a video, the system creates a world.

You can walk through it.

Look around.

Explore.

Talk to other people.

Invite someone into your world.

Travel somewhere that does not exist.

Or allow an AI to continuously transform the environment around you.

The experience is not a video.

It is a world.

---

From Watching Worlds to Entering Worlds

Today's digital media is largely screen-based.

We watch YouTube.

We scroll TikTok.

We browse Instagram.

We play games.

We watch movies.

The screen remains between us and the experience.

Virtual and mixed reality suggest another possibility:

What if the interface disappeared and we entered the experience itself?

Instead of watching a virtual beach, we could walk on it.

Instead of watching a concert, we could stand inside the crowd.

Instead of watching a travel video, we could explore the destination.

Instead of watching someone else's story, we could enter a world together.

---

The Missing Layer

Many of the technologies required for this future already exist in some form.

We have:

- Real-time 3D engines
- VR and XR headsets
- Multiplayer virtual environments
- Procedural world generation
- AI systems
- Generative media
- Digital avatars
- Voice interfaces
- Virtual communities
- Game engines and simulation systems

Open standards such as OpenXR are already working to make XR applications more portable across different devices and platforms.

AI tools are also increasingly being integrated directly into game engines and 3D creation workflows.

But these technologies largely exist as separate pieces.

DreamStream explores what happens when we connect them around one central idea:

«A living, shared, AI-generated world.»

---

From Pre-Designed Games to Living Worlds

Traditional games usually begin with a predefined architecture.

Developers create the rules.

They create the environments.

They define the available objects.

They design the possible interactions.

Players then explore the world that has already been constructed.

DreamStream explores another model.

What if the world itself could become generative?

What if a player could describe a new environment using natural language?

What if an AI world director could modify the environment in real time?

What if the world could continuously evolve according to the people inside it?

Instead of:

Game → predefined world → player

we imagine:

Human intention → AI → evolving world → people

---

Shared Dreams

DreamStream is not only about individual experiences.

We imagine shared experiences.

A creator could become a kind of world streamer.

Instead of streaming a video to an audience, they could invite people into an immersive world.

A host might say:

«"Let's leave the city and travel to another planet."»

The environment changes.

The audience follows.

AI systems generate or modify locations, characters, objects, atmosphere, lighting, sound, and events in real time.

Thousands of people could eventually experience the same evolving world together.

This could create an entirely new form of entertainment:

Live immersive storytelling.

---

A creator might host:

---

The Dream Lab

There is a much more speculative direction beyond immersive VR.

Humans spend hours asleep and many people experience vivid dreams.

Could future technologies eventually interact with dreaming in meaningful ways?

Could immersive environments, dream incubation, lucid-dream research, sensory cues, and future sleep interfaces eventually converge?

Could humans learn to navigate dream experiences more intentionally?

Could shared or socially connected dream experiences ever become possible?

We do not know.

DreamStream does not claim that these technologies exist today.

The Dream Lab is a long-term research question.

It sits at the intersection of:

- Dream research
- Sleep science
- Lucid dreaming
- Neuroscience
- Human-computer interaction
- VR/XR
- Generative AI
- Brain-computer interfaces

The first DreamStream prototypes do not require solving this problem.

But we believe it is worth asking.

The Long-Term Vision

Perhaps one day the boundary between:

watching

playing

creating

exploring

and

dreaming

will become much less rigid.

Perhaps instead of spending our digital lives looking at worlds through rectangles of glass, we will enter them.

Perhaps our devices will become portals rather than screens.

Perhaps creators will become world builders.

Perhaps audiences will become inhabitants.

And perhaps, eventually, the technology will become sophisticated enough to explore the relationship between immersive worlds and human dreaming.

We don't know where this leads.

That's why we're building it openly.

What We Are Building First

DreamStream will begin with the part that is technologically approachable today:

AI-generated shared immersive worlds.

The initial research and development areas may include:

- AI World Generation
- Real-Time 3D
- VR / XR
- Multiplayer Worlds
- AI World Directors
- Natural-Language World Creation
- Digital Avatars
- Live Immersive Streaming
- Procedural Environments
- Creator Tools

The dream-related research will remain a separate experimental track.

---

We Don't Have All the Answers

This project begins with a vision, not a finished technology.

We do not yet know the optimal architecture.

We do not know which technologies will ultimately survive.

We do not know which parts should be centralized or decentralized.

We do not know how far real-time generative worlds can scale.

We do not know what the ultimate interface will be.

And we certainly do not know whether the long-term dream interface is possible.

That's the point.

DreamStream is an open exploration.
